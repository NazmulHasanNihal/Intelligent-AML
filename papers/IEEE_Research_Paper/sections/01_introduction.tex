\section{Introduction}
\IEEEPARstart{F}{inancial} crime surveillance is one of the most critical societal applications of machine learning in modern global financial infrastructure. According to the United Nations Office on Drugs and Crime (UNODC) and the Financial Action Task Force (FATF), illicit syndicates launder between \$800 billion and \$2 trillion annually (representing 2\% to 5\% of global GDP) across traditional banking rails, mobile financial service (MFS) e-wallets, and decentralized crypto-asset ledgers~\cite{unodc2011illicit, fatf2021guidance}. Traditional Financial Intelligence Units (FIUs) continue to rely heavily on static, rule-based heuristics that trigger high volumes of false positive alerts (frequently exceeding 90\% to 95\%), placing a heavy operational burden on human compliance teams.

While Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) have shown immense promise in capturing complex relational topologies~\cite{hu2020heterogeneous, brody2022how, kipf2017semi, hamilton2017inductive, weber2019anti}, deploying GNNs into practical AML surveillance reveals three recurring technical limitations that remain insufficiently addressed jointly:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Continuous-Time Velocity Evasion (Smurfing \& Long-Dwell Dormancy):} Laundering syndicates operate across extreme temporal dynamics, ranging from high-frequency microsecond smurfing bursts to multi-month (30--90+ days) dormant holding chains. Conventional discrete dynamic snapshot models (e.g., EvolveGCN~\cite{pareja2020evolvegcn}) and single exponential decay kernels ($\exp(-\lambda \Delta t)$) fail to capture both regimes simultaneously, causing temporal representations to vanish over extended dwell intervals.
    \item \textbf{Adversarial Topological Camouflage:} Fraudulent entities strategically interleave their illicit flows with high-volume legitimate transactions routed through high-degree merchant, exchange, or utility accounts. In standard GCN and GAT message passing, this adversarial chaff induces severe feature over-smoothing, diluting the anomaly signal and causing illicit nodes to blend into benign commercial hubs~\cite{dou2020enhancing}.
    \item \textbf{The Bayes Risk Imbalance Barrier ($\pi < 0.05\%$):} In real-world enterprise transaction streams, illicit transactions often represent less than $0.05\%$ of total volume. Under such extreme skew, standard loss functions train neural classification heads to output highly conservative probabilities ($0.05 \le \hat{p} \le 0.35$). Under standard decision thresholds ($\tau=0.50$), GNNs suffer from severe recall collapse (missing the vast majority of active money laundering chains).
\end{enumerate}

To address these failure modes, we formulate three core research hypotheses:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Hypothesis 1 ($H_1$):} Synthesizing continuous multi-scale temporal attention with learnable anti-camouflage edge filtering suppresses over-smoothing while maintaining sensitivity to both rapid smurfing bursts and 90-day dormant holding chains.
    \item \textbf{Hypothesis 2 ($H_2$):} Interpolating minority representations directly within latent laundering typology clusters via a parametric bilinear link generator prevents boundary distortion and recovers minority recall without precision collapse.
    \item \textbf{Hypothesis 3 ($H_3$):} Bounding model uncertainty through Class-Conditional Conformal Risk Control (CRC) provides non-asymptotic coverage lower bounds under within-class exchangeability while routing over 99\% of transactions to automated execution tiers.
\end{itemize}

In this paper, we present \textbf{C-STGB} (\underline{C}onformal \underline{S}patio-\underline{T}emporal \underline{G}raph\underline{B}oost), a unified geometric deep learning framework for robust anti-money laundering detection. Our primary contributions are:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Continuous-Time Anti-Camouflage Graph Representation:} A multi-scale continuous attention mechanism combining harmonic logarithmic encodings with burst-aware velocity gates and learnable MLP edge-gated filtration ($g_{ij}$), filtering up to 65.9\% of synthetically injected camouflage linkages under our evaluated adversarial protocol.
    \item \textbf{Typology-Aware Minority Graph Augmentation:} A latent-space GraphSMOTE architecture conditioned on empirical laundering clusters ($\mathcal{C}_k$) with parametric bilinear link generation and Asymmetric Focal Tversky optimization, raising standalone GNN recall on Elliptic-v1 from 10.33\% to 90.10\% $\pm$ 0.72\%.
    \item \textbf{Risk-Controlled Decision \& Operational Triage Layer:} A unified decision framework combining dynamic Bayes threshold calibration ($\tau^*$) with Class-Conditional Conformal Risk Control (CRC) to provide finite-sample coverage guarantees ($\ge 99.0\%$) under within-class exchangeability and an audit-traceable forensic dossier generator for ambiguous boundary cases.
    \item \textbf{Comprehensive 13-Dataset Master Evaluation:} An extensive benchmark spanning 8.81M+ entities and 11.42M+ transactions across blockchain, e-wallet, and fiat banking ledgers, characterizing both high-performing relational domains ($91.42\%$ F1 on Elliptic-v1) and graph observability limits in fragmented multi-bank networks.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Architectural Scoping:} To maintain empirical rigor across 13 benchmarks and 5 independent seeds, this paper focuses on the six core synergistic modules operating in Euclidean spatio-temporal and conformal spaces. Extensions such as Topology-Preserving Weight Regularization (TWP) for lifelong continuous learning across sequential ledgers, non-Euclidean Riemannian hyperbolic geometry, and decentralized privacy-preserving federated graph protocols are delineated as modular future research directions.
