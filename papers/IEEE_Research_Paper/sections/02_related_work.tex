\section{Related Work}

\subsection{Graph Representation Learning for Anti-Money Laundering}
The application of Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) to financial forensic analysis was pioneered by Weber \textit{et al.}~\cite{weber2019anti} on the Elliptic Bitcoin dataset, demonstrating that spatial architectures like Graph Convolutional Networks (GCN)~\cite{kipf2017semi} and GraphSAGE~\cite{hamilton2017inductive} outperform isolated tabular classifiers by capturing relational neighborhood context. Subsequent extensions by Bellei \textit{et al.}~\cite{bellei2024elliptic2} introduced the Elliptic2 multi-asset subgraph dataset, emphasizing the forensic role of multi-hop subgraphs, while Johannessen and Jullum~\cite{johannessen2023predicting} explored heterogeneous graph representations across account ledgers. Beyond Bitcoin, graph embeddings have been investigated for smart contract fraud on Ethereum ledgers by Wu \textit{et al.}~\cite{wu2020phishing} and Zheng \textit{et al.}~\cite{zheng2020xblock}, suspicious trading activity and price manipulation on Mt. Gox by Gandal \textit{et al.}~\cite{gandal2018mtgox}, multi-bank synthetic wire simulations by Lorenz \textit{et al.}~\cite{lorenz2020samld} and Suzumura \textit{et al.}~\cite{suzumura2019amlsim}, and streaming fraud detection by Dal Pozzolo \textit{et al.}~\cite{dalpozzolo2018credit}. However, standard spatial GNNs assume static, homophilic connectivity and remain highly vulnerable to adversarial camouflage, where illicit actors route transactions through high-degree commercial hubs to induce feature over-smoothing~\cite{dou2020enhancing}.

\subsection{Continuous-Time Dynamic Temporal Graphs \& Point Processes}
Financial transactions are inherently continuous-time relational events. Discrete snapshot dynamic GNNs, such as EvolveGCN~\cite{pareja2020evolvegcn}, discretize continuous transaction streams into arbitrary time slices, destroying intra-slice transaction ordering and failing to capture millisecond smurfing bursts. While continuous-time frameworks like Temporal Graph Networks (TGN)~\cite{rossi2020temporal} preserve event order, standard single exponential decay kernels ($\exp(-\lambda \Delta t)$) quickly vanish to zero over 30--90 day dormant holding periods. Self-exciting point processes, mathematically formalized by Hawkes~\cite{hawkes1971spectra}, provide a principled analytical tool to model transaction clustering and velocity acceleration, which we integrate directly into continuous message passing. Furthermore, when deploying dynamic GNNs across non-stationary streaming distributions, catastrophic forgetting emerges as a critical vulnerability, addressed in literature via Topology-Aware Weight Preservation (TWP)~\cite{liu2021overcoming}.

\subsection{Imbalanced Graph Learning \& Latent Oversampling}
Financial transaction graphs exhibit extreme class imbalance, where illicit nodes frequently account for less than $0.05\%$ of total entities. Classical Euclidean oversampling methods (e.g., SMOTE~\cite{chawla2002smote}) operate in raw input space without topology awareness, generating synthetic instances that lack valid relational edges. Under such severe skew, conventional ROC-AUC metrics produce misleadingly optimistic evaluations; hence, Precision-Recall (PR-AUC) analysis is essential for rigorous benchmarking~\cite{davis2006relationship}. To bridge topological oversampling, Zhao \textit{et al.}~\cite{zhao2021graphsmote} proposed GraphSMOTE, performing feature interpolation in the intermediate embedding space. However, unconstrained link generation can inadvertently connect minority synthetic nodes to majority clusters, exacerbating boundary over-smoothing unless conditioned on underlying typology structures.

\subsection{Finite-Sample Conformal Risk Control \& Distribution Shift}
Conformal prediction, originally formalized by Vovk \textit{et al.}~\cite{vovk2005algorithmic} and extended by Angelopoulos and Bates~\cite{angelopoulos2021gentle, bates2021distribution}, provides a mathematically rigorous framework for distribution-free, finite-sample uncertainty quantification. While marginal conformal prediction guarantees valid overall error coverage ($1-\alpha$), it suffers from severe conditional under-coverage on heavily skewed datasets, often generating inflated multi-class prediction sets ($\{0, 1\}$) for up to 18\% of inbound transactions. Furthermore, under temporal distribution shifts common to financial surveillance, standard exchangeability assumptions must be carefully bounded~\cite{tibshirani2019conformal}. In this work, we formulate Class-Conditional Conformal Risk Control (CRC) tailored for extreme financial skew, ensuring finite-sample valid coverage per class under within-class exchangeability while routing over 99.4\% of transactions through automated execution tiers.
